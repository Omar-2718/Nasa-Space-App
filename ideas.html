Dancing with the SARs: pick our idea

NASA Space Apps 2026, Egypt, 24 hours. Challenge: an interactive app that uses NISAR radar data to show the Earth's surface changing.

What we checked: NISAR has data all over Egypt from June to October 2026, with both HH and HV polarizations. Each spot is imaged from the same angle every 12 days, and gets some image about every 4 days. None of the 2024 or 2025 global winners used radar. Egyptian teams won global storytelling and art awards both years. Judging: Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance, Presentation.

Recommended

1. Beneath the Sand

Top pick
What
A slider that shows normal sand on one side and NISAR radar on the other, revealing buried river channels and rock under the sand. Plus the East Owainat farms in the same image, animated over time as crops grow and get harvested.
Story
1981: a Space Shuttle radar finds buried rivers here. 2024: radar finds the lost Nile branch beside the pyramids. 2026: NISAR uses nearly the same wavelength, it's free, and it repeats every 12 days. The ancient rivers left water underground, and today's farms pump it.
Useful for
Choosing where to drill for water, guiding archaeology, and showing which new farmland is active.
Risk
Not yet confirmed that the channels show clearly at full resolution. One 1.6 GB test download settles it.

2. Unmapped Rivers (the discovery version of 1)

Bold
What
Scan the Western Desert with NISAR, trace every channel-like line, compare against published maps of buried rivers, and flag the ones nobody has mapped yet as "candidates".
Useful for
Leads for geologists, water engineers and archaeologists.
Risk
We can't confirm a candidate in 24 hours, so we must call them candidates, not discoveries. It's also harder to build than 1.
Solid alternatives

3. Nile Heartbeat

Safe
What
Water area of Lake Nasser and the Toshka lakes through the 2026 flood season. Water looks dark in radar, so it's easy to map.
Useful for
How much water Egypt stored this year. The Nile supplies about 97% of Egypt's water.
Risk
Other teams may do flood or water maps. Keep it scientific, not political, around the GERD dam.

4. Sinking Delta

High impact
What
Where the Delta coast and Alexandria are sinking while the sea rises, linked to Alexandria's building collapses.
Useful for
Deciding which areas and buildings to inspect or protect first.
Risk
Four months of NISAR may be too short to show slow sinking. We'd need older Sentinel-1 data as backup.

5. Rice Clock

Medium
What
Map rice paddies in the Delta. The data window matches the June to October rice season.
Useful for
Egypt caps rice planting to save water: about 1.07M feddans in 9 governorates in 2025, with fines of EGP 3,000 to 10,000 per feddan.
Risk
The ministry already uses satellites for this, so it scores low on creativity.

6. Dune Watch (new)

Unverified
What
Track sand dunes moving toward roads, farms and villages, for example around Kharga and Toshka. Radar measures how far surfaces shift between passes.
Useful for
Early warning for the people who maintain roads and farms. Fits well as the "change" part of idea 1.
Risk
Dunes may move too little in four months to measure. Not checked yet.
Weaker

7. Desert Heartbeat (farms only)

Weak alone
What
Show every desert farm circle as active or empty over time.
Why weak
Radar can't measure how much underground water is used, so the "so what" is soft. Better as part of idea 1.

8. Megacity Growth

Common
What
Construction in the New Administrative Capital and New Alamein, seen as rising radar brightness.
Why weak
Easy, but not original, and the change in four months is small.

9. Suez Pulse

Dropped
What
Count ships in the canal from radar images.
Why dropped
Ships already broadcast their positions (AIS), so traffic is tracked exactly for free. An image every few days adds nothing, and blockages like 2021 are rare.

Any idea can add sonification, turning the radar signal into sound, as a memorable demo moment. It takes about 2 hours to build.
